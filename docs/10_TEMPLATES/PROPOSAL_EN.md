## 1) Executive summary
A 3–5 week, in-person **summer technical program** in Muscat for **grade 9–11** students, focused on building practical and theoretical skills in drones through four tracks: **theory, flight training, maintenance, and FPV build**.  
The program emphasizes **safety, quality of training, and hands-on experience**, delivered in partnership with government, academic, and private-sector entities, aligned with Oman’s innovation and future-tech directions.

## 6) Program tracks and content

### 6.1 Theoretical training — drone flight
- Flight principles and basic physics  
- Drone types and civil use cases  
- Control systems and air-safety basics  

### 6.2 Practical flight training
- Hands-on control, take-off, and landing  
- Training on safe flight tracks  
- Applied flight scenarios  

### 6.3 Drone maintenance
- Components and architecture of drones  
- Routine inspection and diagnostics  
- Basic repairs and safety procedures  

### 6.4 FPV drone build
- FPV components and system overview  
- Assembly and build steps  
- Basic programming and video/transmission setup  
- First-person-view (FPV) flight experience

## 7) Scope of Work

### 7.1 Strategic Scope Definition
The scope of work for the Summer Technical Drone Program defines the comprehensive framework of activities, responsibilities, and clear boundaries that ensure disciplined execution and measurable outcomes. This scope is designed to be comprehensive enough to achieve strategic objectives, flexible enough to adapt to variables, and controlled enough to guarantee quality and safety.

### 7.2 Comprehensive Scope (Within Scope)

#### 7.2.1 Educational and Training Scope
**Theoretical and Practical Training**
- Design and delivery of an integrated training curriculum covering fundamental principles, practical applications, and technical maintenance.
- Provision of specified training hours for each track (theory, practical, maintenance, manufacturing) to ensure educational objectives are met.
- Implementation of clear assessment standards to measure knowledge and skill acquisition levels.

**Certification and Accreditation**
- Issuance of accredited participation certificates for students who successfully complete the program.
- Documentation of performance and achievements for each trainee to ensure transparency and accountability.

#### 7.2.2 Technical and Operational Scope
**Equipment and Facilities**
- Provision of training drones appropriate for the educational level (beginner to intermediate).
- Provision of FPV equipment for assembly and manufacturing.
- Provision of a safe, well-equipped training environment meeting all safety requirements.

**Technical Support**
- Ensuring equipment readiness at high rates (95%+).
- Provision of specialized technical support during training for immediate troubleshooting.
- Management of technical inventory and spare parts to ensure continuity.

#### 7.2.3 Organizational and Administrative Scope
**Registration and Management**
- Design and implementation of a clear, organized registration system.
- Management of selection and screening processes based on defined criteria.
- Provision of continuous communication with students and parents.

**Safety and Compliance**
- Application of safety protocols approved by the Civil Aviation Authority.
- Ensuring compliance with all relevant regulations and standards.
- Provision of necessary insurance and medical coverage.

### 7.3 Boundaries and Limitations (Out of Scope)

#### 7.3.1 What the Program Does Not Include
**Advanced and Professional Training**
- Training on advanced BVLOS (Beyond Visual Line of Sight) operations.
- Commercial flight training or obtaining professional pilot licenses.
- Training on specialized drone applications (agriculture, surveying, security).

**Long-term Commitments**
- Guaranteeing direct employment for students after the program.
- Providing personal equipment for students to keep after the program.
- Long-term financial or contractual commitments with students or parents.

**Permanent Infrastructure**
- Building permanent training facilities (program uses temporary or rented facilities).
- Purchasing permanently owned equipment (relies on partnerships with drone companies).

### 7.4 Success Criteria and Measurement
**Quantitative Indicators**
- Program completion rate: 85%+ of registered students successfully complete the program.
- Actual training hours: Achievement of 100% of planned hours for each track.
- Assessment pass rate: 80%+ of students pass final assessments.
- Number of assembled drones: Each student participates in assembling at least one FPV drone.

**Qualitative Indicators**
- Student and parent satisfaction level: Minimum 4.0/5.0.
- Training quality as evaluated by partners: "Excellent" or "Very Good".
- Integration and harmony among partners: Positive coordination reports from all parties.

### 7.5 Change Management and Flexibility
**Built-in Flexibility**
- Ability to adapt to schedule changes (weather, emergencies).
- Possibility to modify training content based on feedback from students and trainers.
- Flexibility in number of groups and students according to available resources.

**Change Controls**
- Any change to the core scope requires approval from the steering committee.
- Schedule changes do not affect content quality or safety.
- Any scope expansion must be supported by necessary resources and approvals.

### 7.6 Quality Assurance and Compliance
**Quality Standards**
- Compliance with locally and internationally approved training standards.
- Application of best practices in technical education and practical training.
- Ensuring content aligns with the program's strategic objectives.

**Review and Improvement**
- Weekly performance and progress evaluation.
- Comprehensive final review at program conclusion.
- Documentation of lessons learned and recommendations for future programs.

## 8) Partnerships & Technical Support

### 8.1 Strategic Partnership Vision
The success of the Summer Technical Drone Program relies on an integrated partnership model that brings together government, academic, and private sectors, ensuring the provision of technical resources, specialized expertise, and appropriate regulatory environment. This model is designed to achieve integration between different capabilities and unify efforts toward a common goal: building national technical capabilities in future technologies.

### 8.2 Strategic Partners and Their Roles

#### 8.2.1 Government and Regulatory Partners
**Civil Aviation Authority**
- **Role:** Providing the regulatory framework and necessary approvals for flight training, ensuring compliance with aviation safety standards, and offering specialized technical guidance.
- **Value Added:** Ensuring the program aligns with national and international regulations, and enhancing program credibility through official accreditation.
- **Expected Outcome:** Safe, regulation-compliant training environment, and officially recognized certificates.

**Ministry of Education**
- **Role:** Facilitating access to target students, providing logistical and coordination support with schools, and approving the program as an accredited summer program.
- **Value Added:** Ensuring integration with the national education system, and facilitating registration and selection processes.
- **Expected Outcome:** Broader reach to target students, and official recognition of the program as part of summer development programs.

#### 8.2.2 Academic Partners
**University of Technology and Applied Sciences**
- **Role:** Providing academic expertise in training curriculum design, offering specialized facilities and training spaces (if possible), and overseeing the educational quality of the program.
- **Value Added:** Linking the program to academic standards, providing advanced training environment, and ensuring smooth transition for students from school to higher education.
- **Expected Outcome:** Integrated, academically accredited training curriculum, and opportunities for students to experience the university environment.

#### 8.2.3 Private Sector Partners (Technical & Technical Support)
**Drone Companies (Al Anqa, Ibn Firnas, Sarb, Star Drone)**
- **Role:** Providing training equipment (drones, controllers, safety equipment), offering specialized technical expertise in practical training, and participating in designing practical training tracks.
- **Value Added:** Access to latest technologies and industry practices, practical experience from the actual market, and ensuring training content aligns with industry needs.
- **Expected Outcome:** Modern, practical training experience, and direct link between education and labor market needs.

**Sky Tech (Technical Support & Maintenance)**
- **Role:** Providing specialized technical support in equipment maintenance, troubleshooting, and technical inventory management. This partner ensures equipment continuity and availability throughout the program duration.
- **Value Added:** Ensuring 100% equipment readiness, minimizing downtime due to malfunctions, and providing specialized technical expertise in preventive and emergency maintenance.
- **Expected Outcome:** Reliable training environment, and continuous educational experience without interruption.

**Wakan Tech**
- **Role:** Contributing to advanced technical aspects, especially in programming and electronic systems for FPV drones, and providing support in the manufacturing phase.
- **Value Added:** Access to advanced technologies in programming and embedded systems, and expertise in developing customized technical solutions.
- **Expected Outcome:** Advanced technical outputs, and students capable of understanding complex electronic systems.

**Riyada (Entrepreneurship)**
- **Role:** Providing the framework and resources to support entrepreneurial and innovative aspects of the program, and guiding students toward converting acquired skills into entrepreneurial projects or ideas.
- **Value Added:** Linking technical skills to entrepreneurship opportunities, and providing a platform for outstanding students to develop their projects.
- **Expected Outcome:** A generation of students not only technically skilled, but capable of converting knowledge into economic value.

### 8.3 Cooperation and Coordination Model
The program operates within an organized coordination framework ensuring:
- **Joint Steering Committee:** Includes representatives from key partners to ensure strategic coordination and major decision-making.
- **Specialized Working Teams:** Each partner participates with a specialized team in their field (training, technical, logistical) to ensure smooth execution.
- **Clear Agreements:** Roles and responsibilities of each partner are documented in formal agreements (MoUs) ensuring commitment and transparency.

### 8.4 Mutual Value (Win-Win Model)
**What the Program Offers Partners:**
- Platform to access the next generation of technical talent
- Opportunity to contribute to national development and capacity building
- Enhanced brand image as supporter of innovation and education
- Potential to discover and recruit early talent

**What Partners Offer the Program:**
- Technical resources and specialized equipment
- Practical experience and knowledge
- Regulatory framework and necessary accreditations
- Financial and logistical support

### 8.5 Ensuring Sustainability and Continuity
The partnership model is designed to ensure program sustainability through:
- **Diversification of Support Sources:** Not relying on a single partner, but building an integrated partnership network.
- **Gradual Knowledge Transfer:** Training the local team on technical aspects to ensure future independence.
- **Knowledge Documentation:** Creating a comprehensive knowledge base ensuring program continuity even with partner changes.
